Evaluation Dimensions for Generative AI in Manufacturing

Success must be evaluated through a rigorous engineering framework, not just generic LLM benchmarks

01. Technical Quality Model Focus

  • Extraction Accuracy: F1-score and Precision/Recall on identified entities.
  • Faithfulness Rating: Semantic correctness and hallucination control.
  • Consistency: Stable output replication across repeated API calls.

A labeling tool is useless if label ambiguity corrupts a classifier used for process optimization.

02. Engineering Usefulness Engineer Focus

  • Time Savings: Analysis cycle time reduction from hours to seconds.
  • Context Assembly: Connects disparate historian tags, LIMS, and logs instantly.
  • Actionable Synthesis: Categorizes logs into solvable physical variables.
Downstream PSE Impact
A text-to-SQL tool is useless if it silently queries the wrong tag or time window.

03. Operational Risk Controls Safety Focus

  • Source Grounding: Strict claims verification via direct document links.
  • Negative outcome coverage: Ensuring critical anomalies are not ignored.
  • Validation Sandboxes: Schema checks and unit testing on generated code.
Downstream PSE Impact
A summarization tool is useless if it misses safety-critical caveats or plant limits.

04. Adoption & SME Trust Operator Focus

  • Review Burden: Minimizing human-in-the-loop validation overhead.
  • Coverage of Evidence: High rate of relevant support documentation.
  • Missing Data Alert: Actively flagging gaps rather than guessing content.

Case Study 1: Oil/Water Separation FIPSE-7 Keynote

Case Study 1: Visual Separation Quality Monitoring

The problem: Infrequent, subjective operator checks delay separation process optimization

Process & Operational Context

Separating oil from water is critical in hydrocarbon processing to maintain water quality and prevent environmental off-specs.

  • Sparse Diagnostics: Sampling is performed manually, leading to sparse operational records.
  • Subjective Evaluation: Physical sample quality is assessed visually by operators (e.g. hazy, clear, oily), introducing human bias.
  • Dark Visual Data: Bottle test photos are kept in local folders but not linked to historian tags.
PSE Framing Multi-Variable Constraints
Separation performance is constrained by feed variability, residence time, temperature, chemical treatment, and equipment state. While process historians capture continuous variables, the critical quality label remains visual and qualitative.

Good Separation

Clear water layer, distinct oil-water interface, low residual oil in water. Process variables (demulsifier dose, temperature) are optimal.

Hazy Sample (Transient Warning)

Mild emulsion, cloudy water layer. First indicator of chemical treatment mismatch or feed temperature drops.

Bad Separation (Emulsion Carryover)

Severe interface failure, free hydrocarbons in water layer. Requires rapid corrective action on chemical feed rate and separation residence time.

Case Study 1: LLM-Guided Visual Image Labeling

Using LLM-guided vision models to translate visual inspection photos into structured quality data

The Image Labeling Pipeline

An LLM acts as the visual translation engine, converting bottle test photos into structured parameters for machine learning models.

  • 156 Water Samples: Inspection images gathered during plant trials.
  • LLM Visual Extraction: The model extracts visual features: Color, Haze, Oil Layer presence, and overall Separation Quality.
  • Structured Output: Visual inputs are transformed into a database linked to process operating tags (temperatures, feed rates).
  • Tree-Based Classifier: Structured outputs are used to train XGBoost classifiers mapping process conditions to quality.

Engineering Controls Methodology & PSE Connection

  • Uncertainty Separation: Distinguish visual labeling error from downstream model uncertainty.
  • Validation: Use time-aware validation and class-imbalance tracking to prevent data leakage.
  • PSE Connection: Serves as a bridge from unstructured operational logs to soft-sensing and optimization models.

Case Study 1 Takeaways: Exposing Data Gaps

The primary benefit is not just classification, but mapping plant operating regimes and identifying data limitations

Core Outcomes & Observations

By structuring qualitative images, the modeling pipeline exposed fundamental process and database weaknesses.

  • Label Ambiguity: Mismatches in operator interpretations vs. LLM visual assessments.
  • Sample Imbalance: Plant operates in "Good" state 90% of the time, resulting in sparse "Bad" separation images for classifier training.
  • Temporal Sparsity: Images are collected at irregular time intervals, making dynamic time-series modeling challenging.
  • Actionable Value: Guides plant engineers on where to install inline turbidimeters and triggers active learning loops.

PSE Deployment Active Learning & Process Integration

  • Active Learning: Target future sampling efforts specifically where classifier uncertainty is highest.
  • Causal Constraints: Use process physics and kinetics to bound model-predicted root causes.
  • Closed-Loop Safety: Use visual classifier triggers for operator advisory, avoiding direct autonomous control.

Case Study 2: Complaint RCA FIPSE-7 Keynote

Case Study 2: Customer Quality Complaint Data

The problem: Low-severity quality complaints contain critical signals but are rarely analyzed systematically

Unstructured Complaint Records

Customer complaints typically combine structured batch codes with unstructured narratives written in client-specific language.

  • Cost of Poor Quality (COPQ): Repeated low-severity complaints are costly but get bypassed during formal RCA.
  • Domain Terminology: Translating customer descriptions (e.g. "gels in film", "viscosity drift") to manufacturing parameters is highly manual.
  • Hidden Quality Signal: The key manufacturing cause lies buried in qualitative feedback narratives.
The Translation Gap
Customer complaints describe application failures (e.g. film gels, poor wetting), which must be mapped to process variables (temperatures, reactor residence times) to be actionable.

Complaint Example: Polymer Film

"Customer reports visual haziness and small gel particles during extrusion run of batch #8294. Blocked screen pack filters after 2 hours."

Complaint Example: Coatings & Adhesives

"Product exhibits poor surface wetting and inconsistent cure profiles. Viscosity appears higher than typical for lot #9201."

Complaint Example: Specialty Chemicals

"Yellow discoloration observed in clear liquid samples upon arrival. Trace contaminants or packaging seal integrity failure suspected."

Case Study 2: Extract, Categorize, & Route Workflow

A systematic, LLM-powered pipeline to structure complaint narratives and integrate expert feedback

LLM-Guided Causal Structuring

The workflow uses structured prompts to parse narratives and extract causally distinct fields for downstream analytics.

  • Summarization: Converts long, confusing customer emails into concise summaries.
  • Causal Extraction: Identifies Observed Symptom vs. Hypothesized Cause vs. Confirmed Root Cause.
  • Taxonomy Mapping: Maps free text into fixed engineering categories (e.g., raw materials, reaction settings, packaging).
  • SME Verification Loop: Uncertain extractions are automatically routed to engineers for review, updating the system.

Implementation Rules Taxonomy & PSE Linkage

  • Causal Hygiene: Distinguish observed symptoms from hypothesized and confirmed root causes; forbid hallucinated causes.
  • SME Disagreement: Analyze expert disagreements to identify taxonomy ambiguities and missing documentation.
  • PSE Integration: Links text-based quality categories to numeric variables (batch histories, raw materials, reaction settings).

Case Study 2 Results: SME Alignment & Scaling

Case Study 3: Asset Reliability & Data Reconciliation

The problem: Reliability engineering is bottlenecked by fragmented, unaligned equipment histories

Data Fragmentation Bottleneck

Determining pump or compressor lifetimes requires manual lookup across three separate databases that lack common keys.

  • APM Database: Structured maintenance orders with missing or generic failure codes (e.g. "broke").
  • Technician Logs: Free-text shift comments and handwritten technician repair notes.
  • Historian Data: Time-series process trends (vibration, motor current, pressure drops).
Asset Examples
Examples: Pumps with inconsistent failure modes across sites; heat exchangers where fouling, cleaning cycles, and performance are logged in separate systems; and compressors combining vibration trips, technician logs, and historians.

Case Study 3: Orchestrated Asset Reliability Agent

A multi-tool GenAI system that generates queries, extracts failure details, and executes code

Integrated Agent Capabilities

Rather than just chatting, the LLM orchestrates specialized engineering tools to automate the reliability data workflow.

  • Text-to-SQL: Generates complex SQL queries on-the-fly to pull maintenance history.
  • Failure Code Extraction: Fills in missing EAM failure mode columns by parsing unstructured technician notes.

Guardrails Agent Controls & Reliability Link

  • Data Lineage: Attach source links and extraction confidence ratings to all structured failure details.
  • PSE Connection: Automates context assembly to feed Weibull survival analysis and alarm-state anomaly detection models.

GenAI Risks Are Real Engineering Risks

Hallucinations, drift, and query mistakes can directly compromise safety, quality, and production decisions

01. Hallucination Critical Safety Risk

Plant Example: LLM suggests incorrect maintenance or interlock bypass actions.
Engineering Impact: Safety hazard if operators perform ungrounded procedures.
Mitigation
Strict retrieval constraints limiting answers to validated sources.

02. Wrong Grounding Context Failure

Plant Example: Retrieving outdated startup procedures from decommissioned lines.
Engineering Impact: Valve configuration errors or off-spec batches during reactor startups.
Mitigation
Metadata filtering restricting retrieval to active plant operating files.

03. Query/Tool Failure Interface Risk

Plant Example: Generating SQL queries that pull tag data in psig, assuming psia.
Engineering Impact: Corrupts compressor efficiency calculations and reactor heat balances.
Mitigation
Schema unit verification tests and parameter boundary validation.

04. Automation Bias Human Factor

Plant Example: Operator blindly accepts a generated handover summary, omitting a key bypass.
Engineering Impact: Loss of situational awareness leading to process shutdowns.
Mitigation
Mandatory interactive checklists highlighting safety-critical alerts in the UI.

Adoption Playbook: Responsible Deployment

Move from prototypes to manufacturing systems by selecting high-friction, low-regret use cases

Start Here Assistive

Target high-friction, low-regret tasks where errors are easily reviewed.

Technical Search: Finding and summarizing operating manuals.
Complaint Theming: Categorizing customer defects.
Maintenance Logs: Structuring narrative texts for analysis.
Handover Drafts: Compiling shift logs for engineer review.

Avoid Initially High-Risk

Do not link models to safety-critical decisions without human verification.

Direct Control: Writing setpoints directly to controller loops.
Safety Bypasses: Recommending safety interlock overrides.
Unproven RCA: Relying on LLMs alone for root-cause decisions.
Auto Customer Claims: Auto-generating quality declarations.

Deployment Rules Playbook

Four fundamental guardrails for scaling manufacturing systems.

Human-in-the-Loop: Keep operators as the final approval layer.
Source Traceability: Link model outputs directly to evidence.
Escalation Paths: Flag uncertainty and route tasks to SMEs.
Workflow Integration: Embed models into existing plant portals.

Closing Vision: Integrated Decision Systems

The long-term opportunity: Combining domain expertise, enterprise databases, and fundamental models

Integrated Decision Framework

GenAI’s lasting impact in chemical manufacturing will come from integrated environments where engineering judgment is supported rather than bypassed.

  • Multimodal Support: Combining time-series process tags, narratives, and equipment images.
